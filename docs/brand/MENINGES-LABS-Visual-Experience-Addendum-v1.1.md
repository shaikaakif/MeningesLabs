## 1. The creative correction

The earlier brief did not specify the full level of character-led animation and motion-rich product storytelling present in the supplied Creto screenshots. It covered the high-level site structure and some interactive demonstrations, but did not fully specify:

- A recurring animated brand character or guide.
- Character animation states and how it participates in storytelling.
- Floating interface cards and layered product-preview compositions.
- A short visual walkthrough explaining a business workflow.
- Multi-screen product/interface demonstrations.
- Animated capability or category carousels.
- Kinetic typography, carefully used gradients and decorative motion graphics.
- A consistent motion language reused across the entire experience.

These are now explicit requirements. The goal is not to create a static agency website with a few hover effects. The goal is to create a coherent, responsive, motion-led digital experience that explains complex services in a memorable way.

## 2. Reference analysis: what to learn from Creto

Reference: [Creto](https://creto.app/)

The supplied screenshots show these recurring techniques:

1. **A strong product hero:** large, direct headline, high-contrast accent colour, short supporting copy, floating interface previews and a central product/character visual.
2. **A visible character:** the mascot is not a static decoration; it participates in the visual story and appears in several sections.
3. **An explainer sequence:** a numbered process, a framed walkthrough and cards that make the steps easy to understand.
4. **Layered interface surfaces:** tilted dashboard cards, phone mockups, profile cards, progress indicators and callouts arranged around a central visual.
5. **Feature walkthroughs:** large screenshots paired with short numbered explanations.
6. **Trust and reassurance:** a structured section explaining how the product handles verification, payment or privacy.
7. **Motion graphics:** subtle loops, decorative marks, moving paths, transitions and section-specific animation.
8. **A recognisable design system:** consistent type, bright accent colour, rounded surfaces, icon treatments and repeated character language.

### What MENINGES LABS should borrow

Borrow the **communication techniques**: show rather than merely describe, make the process visual, use a recurring character, reveal interface details progressively, and use motion to direct attention.

### What MENINGES LABS must not borrow

Do not reproduce Creto's fox/cat mascot, its character proportions, costume, face, poses, illustrations, logo, exact card compositions, screenshots, copy, section layouts or distinctive decorative graphics. Do not simply recolour its designs. Do not reproduce its app interface or suggest that MENINGES LABS offers the same creator marketplace or paid-session product.

The new character, interface demonstrations, layouts, motion paths, diagrams and copy must be original to MENINGES LABS and based on its own business proposition.

## 3. Original MENINGES LABS visual concept

### Working creative territory: “Systems in Motion”

MENINGES LABS connects multiple layers of business infrastructure. The website should make that idea visible through a recurring visual system:

- Three interrelated layers, echoing the three folded strokes of the approved ML monogram.
- Independent components that move into alignment and become one connected system.
- Clear flow paths between discovery, acquisition, conversion, automation and measurement.
- Warm, tangible surfaces and a vibrant tangerine identity, balanced with deep technical backgrounds.
- A small, original animated guide that helps explain what is happening.

### Proposed animated brand guide — an original “living monogram”

Create a character-like visual companion unique to MENINGES LABS. It should be derived from the brand's layered/ribbon geometry rather than from an existing animal mascot.

**Core construction:**
- Build the silhouette from three soft, continuous folded forms that reference the approved ML monogram without reproducing the complete logo as a literal body.
- Use a compact, recognisable silhouette that remains readable at small sizes.
- Use tangerine, warm cream and white, with deep-ink details where needed.
- Give it a restrained expressive language: curious, precise, calm, helpful and confident.
- If facial features are used, keep them minimal and geometric. Avoid copying animal ears, tails, clothing, facial proportions or costume styling from Creto.
- The character must remain original in silhouette, material, colour blocking, facial design, animation and poses.

**Character behaviour:**
- Idle: gentle floating/breathing movement; almost still.
- Discover: leans/turns toward an incoming node or new information.
- Connect: guides two or more separated components toward alignment.
- Build: gestures toward a live interface preview or process step.
- Complete: the layers settle into a clean connected composition; a small, restrained acknowledgement.
- Explore: reacts to a selected tab or capability on desktop and to a tap/selection on mobile.

The guide should feel like a small digital studio companion, not a children's cartoon and not a generic robot. It should help explain the system, not become the subject of every section. Keep it out of dense technical copy and long-form article pages.

**Prototype before full production:** create 2–3 silhouette explorations and a simple idle/one-interaction animation first. Confirm that the character is sufficiently distinctive and professional before producing a large animation set. Do not name the character or lock its personality until the visual prototype is approved.

## 4. Homepage — revised high-impact experience

The homepage should feel like a guided demonstration of the MENINGES LABS approach. Use the following narrative sequence.

### 4.1 Hero — “We engineer the systems behind business growth.”

**Layout:**
- Desktop: asymmetric 5/7 or 6/6 split, with headline and copy on one side and a large animated composition on the other.
- Mobile: headline and CTAs first; character and interface composition below, without crowding the first screen.
- Use a warm-paper base with a restrained tangerine/peach radial glow or soft gradient wash behind the visual. Avoid a generic full-page gradient.

**Suggested copy:**
- Eyebrow: `DIGITAL ENGINEERING · AI SYSTEMS · GROWTH INTELLIGENCE`
- H1: `We engineer the systems behind business growth.`
- Supporting copy: `Software, applied AI, automation, search and performance—connected around the way your business works.`
- Primary CTA: `Discuss a project`
- Secondary CTA: `Explore our capabilities`

**Visual composition:**
- Original living-monogram guide as the main visual anchor.
- Three to four floating, real HTML/CSS interface cards around it. Examples: `Digital platform`, `Enquiry workflow`, `Search visibility`, `Campaign measurement`.
- Fine connected paths or moving dots linking these surfaces.
- Use short labels and legible interface previews. Do not bake text into generated imagery.
- Avoid invented revenue, lead counts, rankings, client names or performance claims. Any sample values must be marked `Illustrative demo data`.

**Motion:**
- Character enters with a small, smooth reveal and settles into its idle state.
- Interface cards enter in a deliberate sequence, not all at once.
- One connecting line draws between two cards to communicate the idea of integration.
- Selected keyword in the headline may receive a restrained colour reveal or underline animation.
- No constant high-speed motion, cursor-chasing or large parallax.

### 4.2 The problem — disconnected tools, disconnected work

Create a before/after visual narrative:

- Left/first state: separate, unconnected nodes for website, enquiries, campaigns, CRM, follow-up and reporting.
- Transition: animated paths begin connecting the relevant nodes.
- Right/second state: one coherent system with clear handoffs and measurement.

The visual should explain the value of integration. It must not suggest that every client needs every service or that all tools are automatically integrated without discovery and scope.

### 4.3 Connected capabilities — five service divisions

Use five visually distinct modules, not five identical cards. Each one should have its own mini demonstration and an obvious route to its service page.

1. **Digital Engineering** — website/platform frame, responsive page slice, code-to-interface transition or system architecture preview.
2. **Search Intelligence** — page structure, technical health, local information and content relationships. Avoid fabricated rankings or traffic graphs.
3. **Performance Marketing** — an illustrative campaign-to-landing-page-to-conversion path. Avoid implying guaranteed leads or sales.
4. **AI & Automation** — a node-based workflow with clear human approval/escalation points.
5. **Growth Intelligence** — a prioritisation map or diagnostic board showing how business constraints become a scoped roadmap.

**Interaction:** selecting a module changes the associated preview, explanatory copy and CTA. The selected state must work by mouse, keyboard and touch. Do not rely on hover to reveal essential information.

### 4.4 Process — “From business problem to working system”

Use a numbered, animated process. Recommended five stages:

- `01 Discover` — understand the business, offer, customer journey and current tools.
- `02 Diagnose` — identify visibility, acquisition, conversion or operational bottlenecks.
- `03 Architect` — define the right components, data flow, scope and responsibilities.
- `04 Engineer` — design, build, integrate, test and deploy.
- `05 Optimise` — monitor agreed measures and improve what is in scope.

**Visual treatment:** a single connected path travels through the five stages. The active stage highlights its visual, description and output. On mobile, use a stacked timeline or horizontal snap carousel with clear previous/next controls.

### 4.5 Product-style walkthrough — 30–50 seconds

Create a concise demonstration video or interactive animation titled something like `A connected business workflow`.

Use an illustrative example that is relevant to a real business, without claiming it is a deployed client system. Example sequence:

1. A customer submits an enquiry.
2. The website captures the enquiry.
3. The system classifies and routes it.
4. An AI assistant drafts or sends an approved response, with human escalation where appropriate.
5. The CRM records the interaction.
6. A follow-up or booking step is initiated using actual system availability.
7. A measurement panel shows the event trail.

Clearly label the example `Illustrative workflow`. Use sample data only where necessary and mark it visibly. Do not imply that an integration, live system, customer or outcome exists unless it actually does.

The walkthrough may be implemented as:
- A short muted video with a poster and playback controls; or
- A lightweight interactive sequence built from real HTML/CSS/SVG elements.

Prefer the interactive sequence if it remains accessible and performant. If video is used, provide captions/transcript, pause controls, a poster image and a reduced-motion/static alternative.

### 4.6 Interface showcase — real screens, not fake SaaS claims

Use a composed multi-screen section inspired by Creto's phone mockups, but adapted to the agency's actual offer.

Possible panels:
- Responsive website on desktop and mobile.
- A client portal or SaaS dashboard prototype.
- An automation/workflow builder concept.
- A search/measurement report view.

Requirements:
- Show actual completed work where available.
- Concept UI must carry a clear `Concept preview` or `Illustrative interface` label.
- Never create fake client data, fake testimonials, fake active projects or fabricated performance metrics.
- If no production interface exists for a service, show a clearly labelled conceptual architecture, not a fake screenshot pretending to be live software.
- Use crisp text rendered in HTML/SVG or actual captured screenshots. Never ask an image model to render interface copy.

### 4.7 Proof and selected work

Use a horizontally scrollable or editorial case-study presentation only when real projects and evidence are available.

Each published project should show, where approved:
- Client/project name or anonymised context.
- Business problem.
- Scope delivered.
- Role and responsibilities.
- Technology or services used.
- Verified outcome and time period, if measured.

If case studies are not ready, show a modest `Selected experiments` or `Concept explorations` area and label each item accurately. Do not create invented brand logos or fabricated results to fill space.

### 4.8 Trust, safeguards and working principles

Creto has a trust section because its product involves creator verification, payments and private sessions. MENINGES LABS should not copy those claims. Build a trust section around facts that are relevant and supportable:

- Clear scopes and deliverables.
- Human oversight for consequential AI workflows.
- Transparent third-party and usage costs.
- Secure handling of project credentials and data.
- Documented handover and agreed support boundaries.
- Accurate measurement and honest reporting.

Do not claim certification, encryption standards, compliance, uptime or security audits unless independently established.

### 4.9 FAQ and final invitation

Retain the useful existing FAQ content but improve hierarchy, searchability where needed, accessible disclosure behaviour and answer legibility. Finish with a dark technical or tangerine CTA panel and the full company lockup where space permits.

## 5. Visual system — richer, but controlled

### 5.1 Gradients and backgrounds

Use gradients selectively to create depth and focus:
- Warm orange-to-peach radial wash behind the hero character.
- Very subtle warm-paper/cream tonal transitions between selected sections.
- Deep technical panels can use a restrained navy gradient if it improves depth.
- Small coloured halos may sit behind important interface previews.

Do not put a gradient on every button, card and section. Keep the brand's core orange `#FF7300` recognisable and preserve white/ink contrast.

### 5.2 Floating interface cards

- Use 2–4 cards in a major visual composition, not a dozen random floating rectangles.
- Each card must explain one business capability or workflow state.
- Small tilt or depth offsets may be used, but keep text horizontal and readable.
- Cards should have consistent padding, border, shadow and radius rules.
- Never let a floating card obscure the headline, CTAs or character.
- On mobile, stack cards or convert them to a swipeable carousel; do not keep desktop offsets that cause overflow.

### 5.3 Motion graphics vocabulary

Create a small reusable library of original brand motion assets:
- Layer-fold reveal based on the monogram's three strokes.
- Path-draw animation for workflow connections.
- Node pulse when a process step becomes active.
- Small tangerine accent strokes for section transitions.
- Number/metric count-up only for real, clearly sourced values.
- Soft card lift/settle transitions.
- Masked typography reveal for selected headings.
- Progress indicator for the walkthrough.

Do not animate every number or add confetti by default. Motion should help explain relationships and state changes.

### 5.4 Iconography

Continue one coherent icon family or custom geometric SVG set. Use service-specific icons for the five divisions. Icons must have consistent stroke weight and alignment. Do not use emoji as service icons and do not imitate Creto's exact icon tiles.

## 6. Motion architecture and technical implementation

### Existing stack to preserve

Repository audit indicates:
- Next.js `^16.0.0`
- React `^19.2.0`
- Tailwind CSS `^4.1.0`
- Motion `^12.23.0`

Use the installed Motion package through its React API (`motion/react`) for component transitions where appropriate. Do not replace the framework or add a large animation runtime by default.

### Recommended implementation layers

1. **CSS:** hover/focus states, simple floating, opacity, transform, underline and decorative-loop animations.
2. **Motion for React:** orchestrated entrance, card sequencing, tab transitions, process progress, layout changes and reduced-motion-aware transitions.
3. **SVG:** the original brand guide, layered ribbon shapes, paths, nodes and diagrams where vector geometry is useful.
4. **Video/WebM:** only for high-fidelity character performance or a pre-rendered explanatory demo that cannot reasonably be achieved with DOM/SVG. Always provide poster and controls.
5. **Rive/Lottie:** optional, not mandatory. Introduce only if a real optimised animation file is provided and the extra runtime is justified. Do not install a dependency simply to create a decorative animation.

### Performance constraints

- Animate `transform` and `opacity` where possible; avoid continuously animating layout dimensions or expensive filters.
- Avoid giant blurred layers covering the viewport.
- Lazy-load below-the-fold video and heavy visual modules.
- Respect aspect ratios to prevent layout shift.
- Use `next/image` for raster assets where appropriate.
- Serve responsive image sizes and modern formats.
- Do not autoplay audio.
- Prefer muted, short, looping hero media with a pause/stop option if video is used.
- On mobile or low-power devices, render a simplified character and a static or reduced-motion preview.
- Keep LCP content server-rendered and visible without waiting for animation code.

### Accessibility and reduced motion

- Honour `prefers-reduced-motion` throughout the site.
- Provide a static poster or final state for every non-essential animation.
- No autoplaying motion that cannot be paused where it persists or distracts.
- Ensure tabs, carousels and workflow selectors are keyboard operable.
- Use accessible labels and semantic headings for all interactive previews.
- Do not hide important information in hover-only panels or animation timing.
- Maintain visible focus indicators and WCAG-compliant contrast.

### Suggested motion timings

Treat these as starting tokens, then test in-browser:

| Interaction | Initial range |
|---|---:|
| Button feedback | 140–200 ms |
| Card hover/press | 180–260 ms |
| Tab/preview transition | 220–360 ms |
| Section entrance | 450–700 ms |
| Character idle cycle | 4–8 seconds, subtle |
| Connecting path animation | 700–1,300 ms per sequence |

Avoid long entrance delays that make the page feel slow. Motion must never block reading or interaction.

## 10. Updated acceptance criteria

The rebrand is not complete unless all of the following are true:

### Originality
- The character is original and does not imitate Creto's animal mascot.
- The layouts, interface previews, decorative graphics and animation choreography are distinct.
- No copied wording, assets, exact card compositions or brand marks are present.

### Visual experience
- The homepage has one coherent animated hero composition.
- The character has at least idle and interaction states, plus a static/reduced-motion fallback.
- At least one short product/workflow walkthrough explains an actual MENINGES LABS-style engagement.
- Capability modules have distinct and informative visuals.
- Interface cards are legible, deliberate and responsive.
- Gradients and shadows are selective, not applied indiscriminately.
- The site retains a professional B2B engineering character.

### Accuracy
- All conceptual screens are labelled as concepts or illustrative demos.
- No fabricated client, result, metric, testimonial, certification or security claim appears.
- The service descriptions reflect what MENINGES LABS actually offers.
- All existing public contact details and route URLs are preserved.

### Motion, accessibility and performance
- Reduced-motion mode works.
- Keyboard and touch interactions work.
- No essential content depends on hover or animation.
- Mobile has no overflow or inaccessible overlapping cards.
- Heavy visuals are deferred and do not delay primary content.
- The production build passes.
